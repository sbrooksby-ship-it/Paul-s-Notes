# Division 6 — St. George, Utah Success Story Outreach Campaign (Cohort Project)

**Project Owner:** TBD — not yet assigned

**Status:** Active — in planning/early stage  
**Type:** Cohort project (tested with a defined group before broader rollout)

---

## 📌 Cohort

A pilot group of 2,000 existing Balance of Nature customers who live in or near St. George, Utah.

---

## 🔬 Hypothesis

If we proactively invite customers to share their Success Stories and give them multiple easy submission options, then more customers will participate because they can choose the method that is most convenient for them.

---

## 📏 Measurement Framework (original)

| What we're measuring | How we measure it | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| Email open rate | Email reports | Email and SMS reports |
| Success Stories submitted | Total completed submissions | Success Story Tracker |
| Submission method used | Breakdown by channel (email / web form / SMS) | Website submissions, Success Story Tracker |
| Response rate | Outreach engagement funnel | Email and SMS reports |
| Completed consent statements | Signed consent for marketing use | Submission records |
| Public reviews received | New reviews on Google/Trustpilot | Review platform reporting |

**Data source:** Email and SMS reports, website submissions, Success Story Tracker, and review platform reporting.

**Anticipated End Date:** Sixty days after the first outreach is sent to the 2,000-customer cohort.

---

## ✅ Seven's Assessment

### 1. Proving "more customers will participate"

To prove the campaign actually drove participation, you need to set target conversion rates for your outreach funnel. Without a target, you can't tell whether the results were good or bad.

**The Pilot Metric (Outreach Engagement):** Track the email and SMS open and click-through rates to ensure the message is actually being seen.

**The Target:** Achieve at least a **40% open rate** and a **10% click-through rate** on the initial outreach.

**The Pilot Metric (Story Submissions):** The total number of completed Success Stories submitted by the end of the 60 days.

**The Target:** Generate **100 new Success Stories** — which would be a solid **5% conversion rate** from the total 2,000-person cohort.

---

### 2. Proving they participated "because they could choose the method"

This is the core of the hypothesis. If you offer three methods (e.g., email, web form, SMS) and 99% of people use the web form, your hypothesis is actually incorrect — the multiple options didn't matter. You need to prove that having options captured people who otherwise wouldn't have participated.

**The Pilot Metric (Method Distribution):** Track the percentage breakdown of the submission methods used.

**The Target:** See a **varied distribution across options**, with at least **20% of total submissions coming from the secondary/alternative methods** — proving that offering those alternative methods directly resulted in stories you would have otherwise lost.

---

### 3. Measuring the Secondary Outcomes (Consent & Reviews)

The team member mentioned consent statements and public reviews. These are important business goals, but they should have specific targets tied to the people who submitted stories.

**The Pilot Metric (Consent):** The percentage of submitted stories that include a signed consent statement for marketing use.

**The Target:** **100% completion rate** — if the submission process is truly easy, the consent step shouldn't be a bottleneck.

**The Pilot Metric (Public Reviews):** The number of customers who, after submitting a story, go on to leave a public review.

**The Target:** Convert **15% of the story submitters** (roughly 15 people) into leaving a 5-star public review on Google or Trustpilot during the 60-day window.

---

### Summary — What Success Looks Like at Day 60

By laying it out this way, the team doesn't just have to say "we got some stories and looked at the open rate." At the end of the 60 days, they can confidently say:

> "We hit a 5% submission rate, and because 30% of those came from our new SMS option, we proved that offering multiple methods increases total participation."

Without these specific targets, the project can only report activity, not results.
