**Type:** Cohort project (tested with a defined group before broader rollout)

---

## 📌 Cohort

Customers with two or more payment declines due to insufficient funds over the first 6 months of the year (1/1/26 – 6/30/26).

---

## 🔬 Hypothesis

If we prompt high-decline customers to add a backup payment method, then we will see a decrease in future payment declines and an increase in successful retries, because customers will have an alternative payment option when their primary method fails.

---

## 📏 Measurement Framework

| What we're measuring | How we measure it |
|---|---|
| % of customers who opt in to backup payment method | Number who add a backup method ÷ total prompted cohort |
| Recovery rate of failed payments (retry success) | % of previously declined payments that succeed on retry after backup method is added |
| Revenue recovered from previously declined transactions | Dollar value of transactions that were recovered after backup method was added |

**Data source:** Recharge (backend reporting via CSM) and internal tracker.

## ✅ Seven's Assessment

### 1. Proving customers actually adopted the solution

Instead of just tracking the "% who opt in," you need a concrete conversion goal for the campaign.

**The Metric:** Track the percentage of customers in the cohort who successfully add a secondary card to their Recharge profile after receiving the prompt.

**The Target:** Achieve a **15% opt-in rate** among the contacted cohort by the end date (adjust this number based on your average email conversion rates).

---

### 2. Proving it increased successful retries (the core hypothesis)

Instead of a vague "recovery rate," you need a clear benchmark against their past behavior — since you already know these customers historically fail payments.

**The Metric:** Compare the automated failed-payment recovery rate of this cohort during the testing window against their **historical recovery rate from the first half of the year (1/1 – 6/30)**.

**The Target:** **Increase the successful retry rate by 20 percentage points** for the customers who added a backup method — proving the backup card directly saved the transaction.

---

### 3. Proving the financial impact

Instead of just listing "revenue recovered," set a hard dollar or percentage goal to show the business value of this project.

**The Metric:** Total monthly recurring revenue (MRR) successfully processed specifically through the new backup payment methods.

**The Target:** Recover a **minimum of $5,000 in revenue** (insert a realistic dollar amount based on your product cost and cohort size) that would have otherwise churned due to insufficient funds.

---

### What success looks like at the end

By laying it out this way, the team member has taken a great set of KPIs and turned them into a **pass/fail test**. If they hit the revenue target and the recovery rate increase, they have the hard proof they need to suggest rolling this feature out to the entire customer base.

Without these specific targets, the project can only report activity, not results.
