# Division 3 — 3-Month Bundle Subscription for New Plus Members (Cohort Project)

**Project Owner:** Sherry  
**Division:** 3  
**Status:** Active — in planning/early stage  
**Type:** Cohort project (tested with a defined group before broader rollout)

---

## 📌 Cohort Goal

Increase customer purchase options by introducing a 3-month bundle subscription, while driving higher upfront revenue, improving AOV, and supporting stronger retention and fulfillment efficiency.

---

## 👥 Cohort

New Plus Member customers only, acquired through digital channels (paid social).

---

## 🔬 Hypothesis

A 3-month bundle offer to new social customers will increase AOV, attract higher-intent buyers, improve 60–90 day retention, and create a more scalable path to higher LTV customers.

---

## 📏 Measurement Framework (original)

| What we're measuring | How we measure it |
|---|---|
| Conversion Rate | Cohort conversion from paid social impression/click to Plus Member purchase |
| Impressions, Calls, Traffic Volume | Paid social platform reporting + site traffic |
| AOV & ROAS | Average order value and return on ad spend for the cohort |
| 60–90 Day Retention | Retention rate at 60 and 90 days post-purchase |
| Net Revenue per Order | Net revenue per order for the cohort |
| Refund & Chargeback Rates | Refund and chargeback rate for the cohort |

## ✅ Seven's Assessment

### 1. Proving it "increases AOV and upfront revenue"

Because they are selling a 3-month bundle, the upfront revenue will naturally be higher. The true test is whether this offsets any potential drop in total conversion rate.

**The Metric:** Blended Average Order Value (AOV) and Net Revenue per Order at checkout during the 5-day test.

**The Target:** Increase initial AOV by **[insert target % or $ amount]** compared to the historical baseline of standard monthly subscription sign-ups from paid social.

---

### 2. Proving it "attracts higher-intent buyers and improves 60–90 day retention"

Locking people in for 3 months automatically forces a 90-day lifespan, so "retention" here really means what happens when that 3-month period ends and they are up for renewal in December.

**The Metric (Retention):** The percentage of the cohort that successfully renews for a second billing cycle at the 90-day mark.

**The Target:** The 3-month bundle cohort achieves a **Day-90 retention rate of [insert target %]**, outperforming the standard monthly subscriber Day-90 retention baseline.

**The Metric (Intent/Quality):** Refund and chargeback rates during the first 30 days.

**The Target:** Keep refund/chargeback rates **under [insert target %]**, proving customers understood they were buying a bundle and didn't purchase by mistake.

---

### 3. Proving it is a "scalable path"

For this to be scalable, the cost to acquire these bundle customers cannot wipe out the extra revenue they bring in. (This is where you drop Impressions/Traffic and focus purely on efficiency.)

**The Metric:** Conversion Rate and Return on Ad Spend (ROAS).

**The Target:** Maintain a **conversion rate of [insert target %]** and achieve a **minimum ROAS of [insert target ratio, e.g., 2.0x]** during the 5-day initial test.

---

### What success looks like — a clear scorecard

By grouping it this way, you give the team member a clear scorecard:

- **On Day 6:** Check Section 1 and Section 3 to see if the AOV and ROAS targets were hit.
- **In December:** Check Section 2 to see if the retention target held up.

Without these specific targets, the project can only report activity, not results.
