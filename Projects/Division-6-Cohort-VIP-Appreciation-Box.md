# Division 6 Cohort Projects

*Owned by Rykker*

---

## 📌 Cohort Goal (both projects)

Learn what has kept our most loyal customers connected to Balance of Nature, and test whether personal recognition strengthens retention, satisfaction, and advocacy.

---

## Project 1: VIP Appreciation Box

**Cohort:** Long-term customers with consistent monthly orders who have shown strong loyalty to Balance of Nature AND have shared a Success Story.

**Hypothesis:** If we recognize these loyal customers with a personalized VIP appreciation box, then they will feel more valued, remain engaged, and be more likely to promote Balance of Nature because the experience strengthens their emotional connection to the company.

**Measurement:** Survey completion, customer feedback, engagement, referrals, and additional Success Stories.

**Data source:** Survey results tracker, Calls with customers, and engagement.

**Anticipated End Date:** Thirty days after all ten VIP boxes have been delivered and customer follow-up has been completed.

**Cohort size:** 10 VIP boxes.

### Seven's Assessment

1. **"Feel more valued"** — Instead of sending an automated survey to just 10 people, treat this as a high-touch VIP experience. The pilot metric: a customer success rep makes a personal phone call to all 10 recipients a week after delivery. Target: successfully speak with at least 6 out of 10 customers and document their direct verbal feedback (looking for phrases indicating surprise, delight, or feeling appreciated).

2. **"Remain engaged"** — With only 10 people, just one person canceling their subscription drops retention by 10%. The pilot metric: track the exact subscription status of these 10 specific accounts. Target: zero churn. These are already the most loyal customers — expectation should be that all 10 maintain their monthly orders for the next 3 to 6 months.

3. **"Promote Balance of Nature"** — Include a referral mechanism or social sharing prompt in the box, but keep goals realistic for 10 people. Pilot metric (referrals): generate 2 net-new customer orders from the unique referral codes included in the 10 boxes.

## Project 2: Featured Success Story Customer Recognition

**Cohort Goal:** Test whether personally recognizing customers who share outstanding Success Stories strengthens appreciation, engagement, and loyalty.

**Common factor of cohort:** Customers whose Success Story was voted number 1 for the week by BON employees.

**Hypothesis:** If we personally thank each Featured Success Story customer and provide a small appreciation gift, then the customer will feel more valued and connected to Balance of Nature because their experience was personally recognized.

**Measurement:** Customer response, feedback about the recognition, acceptance of the appreciation gift, online reviews, or additional Success Stories.

**Data source:** Weekly voting results.

**Anticipated End Date:** Four weeks after the first Featured Success Story customer is contacted.

### Seven's Assessment

**The measurement gap: no baseline.** The current measurement framework has no baseline of what these customers were feeling *before* they got the thank you note / gift. Without that baseline, you cannot prove the recognition *inspired* change — you can only show that something happened after the recognition. That's a correlation problem, not a causation problem.

This matters because the hypothesis is explicitly causal: "the customer will feel more valued and connected *because* their experience was personally recognized." If you can't measure what they felt before, you can't prove the "because."

**What a baseline would look like:** Before each Featured Success Story customer is contacted, capture a quick read on where they stand — e.g., "Are you currently happy with your experience with Balance of Nature? Why or why not?" or a one-question pulse check on their connection to the brand. That gives you a before-state to compare the after-state against.

**Without a baseline, the best you can prove is:** these customers responded positively after getting the thank you note / gift. That's useful, but it doesn't answer the hypothesis. It just shows correlation — they were recognized and then responded. You'd need the before-state to claim the recognition *caused* the response.

**Open question for Rykker:** Is there any existing data on these customers before they become Featured Success Stories? If they've already shared a Success Story, they've already demonstrated high engagement — that itself is a kind of baseline. The hypothesis is about feeling, so the baseline should ideally measure feeling.
