# Division 6 — Featured Success Story Customer Recognition (Cohort Project)

**Project Owner:** Rykker  
**Division:** 6  
**Status:** Active — in pilot/early stage  
**Type:** Cohort project (tested with a small group before company-wide rollout)

---

## 📌 Cohort Goal

Test whether personally recognizing customers who share outstanding Success Stories strengthens appreciation, engagement, and loyalty.

---

## 👥 Cohort

Customers whose Success Story was voted number 1 for the week by BON employees.

---

## 🔬 Hypothesis

If we personally thank each Featured Success Story customer and provide a small appreciation gift, then the customer will feel more valued and connected to Balance of Nature because their experience was personally recognized.

---

## 📏 Measurement Framework

| What we're measuring | How we measure it | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| Customer response | Feedback about the recognition | Customer responses / follow-up |
| Feedback about the recognition | Direct customer feedback on the thank-you / gift | Customer responses / follow-up |
| Acceptance of the appreciation gift | Whether they accepted the gift | Delivery / follow-up records |
| Online reviews | New or updated online reviews after recognition | Review platforms |
| Additional Success Stories | Whether they share another Success Story after being recognized | Success Story submissions |

**Data source:** Weekly voting results.

**Anticipated End Date:** Four weeks after the first Featured Success Story customer is contacted.

---

## ✅ Seven's Assessment

**The measurement gap: no baseline.**

The current measurement framework has no baseline of what these customers were feeling *before* they got the thank you note / gift. Without that baseline, you cannot prove the recognition *inspired* change — you can only show that something happened after the recognition. That's a correlation problem, not a causation problem.

This matters because the hypothesis is explicitly causal: "the customer will feel more valued and connected *because* their experience was personally recognized." If you can't measure what they felt before, you can't prove the "because."

**What a baseline would look like:** Before each Featured Success Story customer is contacted, capture a quick read on where they stand — e.g., "Are you currently happy with your experience with Balance of Nature? Why or why not?" or a one-question pulse check on their connection to the brand. That gives you a before-state to compare the after-state against.

**Without a baseline, the best you can prove is:** these customers responded positively after getting the thank you note / gift. That's useful, but it doesn't answer the hypothesis. It just shows correlation — they were recognized and then responded. You'd need the before-state to claim the recognition *caused* the response.

**Open question for Rykker:** Is there any existing data on these customers before they become Featured Success Stories? If they've already shared a Success Story, they've already demonstrated high engagement — that itself is a kind of baseline. But it's a behavioral baseline (they took an action), not an attitudinal one (how they *felt*). The hypothesis is about feeling, so the baseline should ideally measure feeling.
