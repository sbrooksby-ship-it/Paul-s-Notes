# How to Get the Right Check-In Question

*Use this when you don't know what to ask someone. Paul pulls from their profile's check-in questions, recent context, and what's outstanding.*

---

## How to use

Ask me any of these:
- **"What should I ask Michael today?"**
- **"Give me a check-in question for Sophia."**
- **"What do I say to Whitney?"**
- **"What question should I start with for [person]?"**
- **"Give me 3 check-in questions for [person]."**

I'll pull from:
1. Their profile's "Check-In Questions That Work" section (if it exists)
2. What's outstanding between you and them (from Action Items / Pulse)
3. Recent context (last meeting summary, current blockers, what you owe them)
4. Their known working style and preferences

---

## What I'll consider for each person

- Outstanding: agent list (pending since Sep 21), bi-weekly schedule

### Sophia

- Prefers structured work — frame questions around what she's done, not big open "what do you want to do?"
- Sheets corruption is a real ongoing pain — ask about it specifically
- Keeps hobbies/work separate — don't push work questions into personal space

### Whitney
- Proactive, engaged — she'll bring you results rather than waiting
- Idioms initiative is her project — ask about test results, agent patterns
- If results are good, the follow-up question is "what's next — roll out to more agents?"

### Cedric
- EVP, ~1 month response time — don't expect fast replies
- Owns the IT/dev company our company contracts through — his team is slow, lots of people unhappy
- When he does engage, he asks technical questions — be specific and concrete
- Has a conflict of interest on the database decision
- If you go Supabase/self-hosted, you don't need his team

### Adrien
- VP of Division 5 — one step above Brad (your direct boss)
- Owes you a recording of your last meeting — he asked if you had it
- If you need something from him, be direct and specific — he's senior, busy
