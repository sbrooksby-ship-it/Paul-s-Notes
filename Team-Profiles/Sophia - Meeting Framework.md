# Sophia — Meeting Framework

*What to ask / listen for in every meeting with Sophia. Built from her first 1-on-1. Use this to structure your conversations and make sure you're gathering the data that keeps her profile alive.*

---

## Core Questions (Ask Every Check-In)

These are the data points that keep her profile current. Rotate them but hit each one regularly.

### 1. Call Eval Work

> "How are the call evals going this week? Any calls that stood out — good or bad?"

*Why:* This is her core work. Starting here grounds the conversation in what she actually does day-to-day.

**What to capture:**
- Any calls that stood out (good evaluations, problematic calls)
- Her experience with the grading process
- Any feedback she's giving to callers

### 2. The Spreadsheet / Database Situation

> "Any data issues this week? Spreadsheets acting up, names disappearing, anything I need to fix?"

*Why:* This is the ongoing operational problem. Google Sheets is corrupting data, Seven restores previous versions. It may or may not be affecting Sophia's day-to-day, but it's a real problem in her workflow.

**What to capture:**
- Did corruption happen this week? How often?
- Is it getting better or worse?
- Any new incidents?
- Her perspective on it (she notices it, doesn't fix it)

### 3. What She Enjoys (Structured Work)

> "What's something structured/repetitive you worked on this week that you actually enjoyed?"

*Why:* Sophia enjoys the "boring" repetitive work that others find tedious. Asking this shows you understand her work style and value it. She'll likely have an answer.

**What to capture:**
- What specific task/task type she enjoyed
- Why she enjoyed it (the repetitive nature, the detail, the consistency)
- Any pattern — is there a type of call eval or validation she particularly likes?

### 4. Process / Blockers

> "Anything you've noticed that could be improved — processes, tools, anything at all? Even small stuff."

*Why:* Sophia didn't express active frustration in the intro meeting, but she's aware of the spreadsheet problem. She may not volunteer pain points unless asked directly. Similar to Michael in that regard, though for different reasons — Sophia is more reserved, Michael doesn't complain by nature.

**What to capture:**
- Any process friction she's noticed
- Tool issues
- Anything that's making her work harder than it needs to be

### 5. Meeting Structure / Running Things

> "How's the new meeting structure feeling? You and Sam running things — how's that going?"

*Why:* She took charge of tomorrow's meeting and seemed happy to. This is a development opportunity — giving her ownership. Check in on how it's going.

**What to capture:**
- How the meeting went
- What she learned from running it
- Any feedback on the process
- Does she want more ownership? Less?

### 6. Innovation / AI (Careful Here)

> "Any new tools or processes you've come across that you think could make your work easier?"

*Why:* **Don't push AI on Sophia the way you would on Michael.** She explicitly wants hobbies and work separate. Pushing "learn AI at work" crosses her boundary. But she may have ideas about tools or processes that would help. Let her bring it up.

**What to capture:**
- Any tools or processes she's noticed that could help
- Her openness to new approaches (or lack thereof)
- If she does express interest in something specific, follow up

### 7. Work/Life Balance

> "How's everything outside work? Still keeping things separate the way you like?"

*Why:* She values the boundary between work and hobbies. Checking in shows you respect it. Also gives you a human connection point without prying into her actual hobbies (she didn't share them).

**What to capture:**
- She still feels the boundary is healthy
- Any changes in her life outside work that might affect her work (without prying)

## Questions That Don't Work Well

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| "What creative solutions have you thought of?" | She explicitly prefers structure over creative freedom. This would confuse her or make her uncomfortable. |
| "Want to learn some AI to make your job more exciting?" | Crosses her work/life boundary. She keeps hobbies separate from work. Frame AI as a work-efficiency tool, not "fun learning." |
| "What hobbies do you have?" (direct) | She didn't share them in the intro. She may not want to talk about them at work. Let her bring it up if she wants. |

## Meeting Structure Suggestion (20-30 min)

1. **Warm-up (2 min)** — casual, how's the week been
2. **Call eval work (5 min)** — what she's been evaluating, any standout calls
3. **Spreadsheet/data issues (5 min)** — check on the corruption problem
4. **What she enjoyed (3 min)** — structured work she liked this week
5. **Process / blockers (5 min)** — anything friction, anything she's noticed
6. **Meeting ownership (3 min)** — how's running things going, any questions
7. **Your updates / promises (3 min)** — what you're following up on, what you owe her
8. **Forward look (2 min)** — what's coming up

## Red Flags to Watch For

- **Data corruption getting worse** — If the spreadsheet issue is happening more frequently, the database solution becomes more urgent. Track frequency.
- **Silence on process issues** — If she says "everything's fine" repeatedly without being asked directly about specific pain points, probe once more. She's aware of the spreadsheet problem but may not flag other issues.
- **Meeting ownership not going well** — If she struggles with running tomorrow's meeting, don't take it away immediately. Check in, offer support, see if it's a skill gap or a confidence gap.
- **AI pushback** — If you push AI learning on her and she pushes back, back off. She's told you clearly that hobbies and work stay separate. Respecting that builds trust.
- **Cedric's in-house build making progress** — If Cedric's team starts delivering, the Supabase path may become moot. Stay aware of what's happening on that front.
