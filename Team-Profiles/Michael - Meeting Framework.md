# Michael — Meeting Data Framework

*What to ask / listen for in every meeting with Michael. Built from his intro meeting profile. Use this to structure your conversations and make sure you're gathering the data that keeps his profile alive.*

---

## Core Questions (Ask Every Check-In)

These are the data points that keep his profile current. Rotate them but hit each one regularly.

### 1. Success & Metrics

> "What does success look like for you this week?"

*Why:* Michael defined success as **consistency + daily productivity + quantified output**. He wants numbers. Ask him what metrics he's tracking against. This gives you data to put in his profile and shows you respect his definition.

**What to capture:**
- What numbers/metrics is he measuring himself against?
- Is he hitting them? By how much?
- Any gap between his targets and reality?

### 2. Pain Points / Blockers

> "Anything frustrating you this week? Processes, tools, anything I should know about?"

*Why:* Michael **doesn't naturally complain**. He'll bring solutions but may not flag problems unless asked directly. This is the #1 data gap to fill. Make it a standing question.

**What to capture:**
- Specific tools, processes, or policies causing friction
- How long has it been going on?
- Has he already tried to work around it?
- Severity — is it a "nice to fix" or "this is costing me time every day"?

### 3. Hours / Capacity

> "How are the hours looking? Still under that 42 cap?"

*Why:* Brad Wilson set a hard expectation. You're responsible for monitoring. This is both a compliance question and a wellness check — if he's running hot, something may be wrong.

**What to capture:**
- Actual hours this week
- Trend — is he consistently near 42? Under? Over?
- If over: why? (volume, inefficiency, something blocking him?)

### 4. Openness to Projects

> "Is there anything you've been wanting to get involved in outside your core role? You mentioned being available."

*Why:* Michael said he's a "yes person" and available for projects. This is both a development opportunity for him and a way for you to leverage his willingness. Capture any ideas he has.

**What to capture:**
- What projects interest him?
- What skills does he want to develop?
- Any specific ask he's been holding back?

### 5. Win of the Week

> "What's something you're proud of this week?"

*Why:* Every profile needs wins. Michael defined success through productivity — ask him to point to a specific win. This feeds his long-term Wins section and gives you material for recognition.

**What to capture:**
- Specific accomplishment
- Impact / why it mattered
- Any metric attached

## Relationship / Rapport Builders

These aren't data-gathering questions — they're trust-builders. Use them naturally.

- **"How's the Utah idea going?"** — He mentioned it casually. Don't pry, but it's a human hook.
- **"What's one thing I could do to make your job easier this week?"** — Opens the door for things he might not otherwise say.
- **"I saw [X] — how did that go?"** — Reference something specific from his work.
- **"Anything I can take off your plate?"** — Since he's a "yes person" who takes on everything, check if he's overloaded.

## Meeting Structure Suggestion (30 min)

1. **Warm-up (2 min)** — casual opener, rapport
2. **Wins (5 min)** — what went well, what he's proud of
3. **Blockers (10 min)** — what's in the way, what's frustrating
4. **Metrics / goals (5 min)** — what he's tracking, progress
5. **Your updates / promises (5 min)** — what you're following up on, what you owe him
6. **Forward look (3 min)** — what's coming up, anything he wants to get involved in

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

- **Silence on blockers** — If he says "nothing's frustrating" two meetings in a row, probe harder. He may be optimizing for not complaining.
- **Hours creeping up** — Brad's 42-hour cap. If he's consistently near or over, something needs to change (volume, efficiency, or scope).
- **"Yes" to everything** — Michael is a "yes person." Watch for overloading. Check capacity explicitly.
- **AI/tool complaints** — He joked about "AI stuff breaking all the time." If it comes up again, it's not a joke — it's a real friction point.
