## 📋 Projects Owned

### 1. VIP Appreciation Box — Cohort Project (Division 6)

- **Cohort goal:** Learn what keeps our most loyal customers connected to Balance of Nature, and test whether a VIP appreciation experience strengthens retention, satisfaction, and advocacy.
- **Cohort:** Long-term customers with consistent monthly orders who have shown strong loyalty AND have shared a Success Story.
- **Hypothesis:** If we recognize these loyal customers with a personalized VIP appreciation box, they'll feel more valued, remain engaged, and be more likely to promote Balance of Nature — the experience strengthens their emotional connection to the company.
- **Measurement:** Survey completion, customer feedback, engagement, referrals, and additional Success Stories. Data sources: survey results tracker, calls with customers, engagement data.
- **Anticipated end date:** 30 days after all 10 VIP boxes have been delivered and customer follow-up has been completed.
- **Cohort size:** 10 VIP boxes.

**Seven's assessment (Sep 30, 2026):**

1. **"Feel more valued"** — Instead of an automated survey to 10 people, treat this as a high-touch VIP experience. The pilot metric: a customer success rep makes a personal phone call to all 10 recipients one week after delivery. Target: speak with at least 6 of 10 and document direct verbal feedback (looking for phrases showing surprise, delight, feeling appreciated).

2. **"Remain engaged"** — With only 10 people, one cancellation drops retention by 10%. The pilot metric: track the exact subscription status of these 10 specific accounts. Target: zero churn. These are already the most loyal customers — expectation is all 10 maintain monthly orders for next 3–6 months.

3. **"Promote Balance of Nature"** — Include a referral mechanism or social sharing prompt in the box, but keep goals realistic for 10 people. Pilot metric (referrals): generate 2 net-new customer orders from the unique referral codes in the 10 boxes.

### 2. Featured Success Story Customer Recognition — Cohort Project (Division 6)

- **Cohort goal:** Test whether personally recognizing customers who share outstanding Success Stories strengthens appreciation, engagement, and loyalty.
- **Cohort:** Customers whose Success Story was voted #1 for the week by BON employees.
- **Hypothesis:** If we personally thank each Featured Success Story customer and provide a small appreciation gift, then the customer will feel more valued and connected to Balance of Nature because their experience was personally recognized.
- **Measurement:** Customer response, feedback about the recognition, acceptance of the appreciation gift, online reviews, or additional Success Stories. Data source: weekly voting results.
- **Anticipated end date:** Four weeks after the first Featured Success Story customer is contacted.

**Seven's assessment (Sep 30, 2026):**

**The measurement gap: no baseline.** The current measurement framework has no baseline of what these customers were feeling *before* they got the thank you note / gift. Without that baseline, you cannot prove the recognition *inspired* change — you can only show that something happened after the recognition. That's a correlation problem, not a causation problem.

This matters because the hypothesis is explicitly causal: "the customer will feel more valued and connected *because* their experience was personally recognized." If you can't measure what they felt before, you can't prove the "because."

**What a baseline would look like:** Before each Featured Success Story customer is contacted, capture a quick read on where they stand — e.g., "Are you currently happy with your experience with Balance of Nature? Why or why not?" or a one-question pulse check on their connection to the brand. That gives you a before-state to compare the after-state against.

**Without a baseline, the best you can prove is:** these customers responded positively after getting the thank you note / gift. That's useful, but it doesn't answer the hypothesis. It just shows correlation — they were recognized and then responded. You'd need the before-state to claim the recognition *caused* the response.

**Open question for Rykker:** Is there any existing data on these customers before they become Featured Success Stories? If they've already shared a Success Story, they've already demonstrated high engagement — that itself is a kind of baseline. But it's a behavioral baseline (they took an action), not an attitudinal one (how they *felt*). The hypothesis is about feeling, so the baseline should ideally measure feeling.
